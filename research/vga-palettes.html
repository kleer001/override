The same EXEC screen, one hardware generation on: a 1987 VGA monitor, 256 colours on screen at once from an 18-bit DAC. CGA gave us flat cells; VGA gives us ramps — so the intrusion can carry real temperature, the terrain can be shaded, and depth stops being a glyph trick. Every colour below is snapped to VGA's 6-bits-per-channel palette.

The upgrade — VGA, 256 from 262,144

Bench
Read-out. Open ground is still black — an unburned cell is empty machine. What changed from the CGA study is shading: terrain is filled from a low-frequency ramp so walls and bedrock read as lit surfaces, and the burn samples a fire ramp — frontier @ runs white-hot, the settled core # cools to deep coal — so heat is legible without a single number. Glyphs and layout are untouched; this stays a paint pass, just with a lookup that ramps on cell position instead of a flat colour. Colours quantise to VGA's 18-bit DAC (64 levels per channel).
